Compilers know this problem. They move values between registers, all at once, with one move at a time.

One temporary for every cycle. For tool calls, one snapshot.

In general, finding the fewest snapshots is NP-hard.

How fast can a batch run? In rounds: the calls of a round run together.

The fewest rounds are the longest chain of readers and writers.

Better still, start every call as soon as it may. Then the batch takes as long as its slowest chain.

We ran every order of ten thousand random batches.

Forty-two percent were independent. Six percent had a cycle, and needed a snapshot.

A harness that orders the calls needs nine snapshots in ten fewer than one that runs everything at once.
