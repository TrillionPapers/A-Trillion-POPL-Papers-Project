Most of this can be had again, at a price.

A file can be read again. A finding can be derived again, if its premises are at hand.

But what the user said cannot be had again. It was said once.

The same goes for a decision, or for the output of a command that changed something.

For everything else, there is a choice: hold it, or have it again later.

Holding costs tokens at every step. Having again costs a call, or some thinking.

Having a finding again means having its premises too. One choice forces others.

The cheapest way through all these choices is a minimum cut.

Hold an idle file until the holding has cost as much as reading it again. Then let it go.

That rule stays within a factor of two of the best possible.

We compared six rules on two thousand invented sessions.

Keeping the newest half lost something needed in forty-eight percent of them.

The cut, plus everything that cannot be had again, cost eight percent more than the cheapest. And it was safe against every surprise.
