# Congruence closure with unique fixed points

Congruence closure decides which equations between ground terms follow from given ones. Over rational terms, the term graphs with cycles that stand for regular infinite trees, equations have a second source: two terms that solve the same guarded system of equations are equal. This is the rule of unique fixed points, and bisimulation is the way to use it. The idea is to decide which equations follow from given ground equations in all iterative algebras, the algebras in which every guarded system has exactly one solution. The procedure alternates two closures until nothing changes: congruence closure, a least fixed point, and the search for a bisimulation up to the classes found so far, a greatest fixed point. A paper would give the algebraic statement, soundness and completeness of the alternation, its complexity, and two uses. One is equality of equirecursive types under assumed equations when type constructors are not injective, as with abstract types and type functions. The other is a rebuild step for e-graphs with cycles that also merges the classes of equal infinite terms.

What we know on 22 November 2026. Bisimulation up to congruence is the method of [Bonchi and Pous (POPL 2013)](https://doi.org/10.1145/2429069.2429124) for automata, where the congruence comes from the structure of the state space. Sequential equivalence checking of circuits finds signals that agree in two designs by a fixed point that looks like the same alternation ([van Eijk, 2000](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~alanmi/courses/2008_290A/papers/vaneijk_tcad00.pdf)); we have not checked how close it is. A search for e-graphs with a coinductive rebuild found only remarks that cyclic e-graphs are poorly supported. The doubt is that the result is known under another name in one of these fields, and that it is the size of a short conference paper.
